# Adult ADHD assessment: what to expect at each stage

Adults who seek an ADHD assessment are often trying to make sense of a long pattern rather than a sudden change. They may have struggled with focus, organisation, impulsivity, restlessness or overwhelm for years without a clear framework for understanding it.

At Nova Clinics, the adult ADHD pathway is designed to look at those patterns carefully through questionnaires, clinical interview, informant input, QbCheck, multidisciplinary review and follow-up support.

## Why adult ADHD assessments look at both history and current impact

ADHD is not diagnosed on the basis of being busy, forgetful or distracted now and then. Clinicians need to understand whether attention-related and impulse-related difficulties are persistent, whether they were present over time, and how they affect daily functioning.

That is why the pathway combines self-report with informant input and structured assessment tools.

## 1. Online questionnaires

The process begins with questionnaires completed by you and by someone who knows you well.

Nova Clinics asks for background information from both of you so the clinical team can begin forming a picture before the live appointments. In the adult workflow, the broader social context questionnaire section has been removed, so the focus is on your own forms and your informant’s input.

This stage helps clinicians identify the main themes to explore further in interview.

## 2. Personal interview and QbCheck

Your main clinical appointment includes an interview focused on strengths, needs and ADHD-related traits in day-to-day life.

Nova Clinics uses a DIVA/ACE-informed approach to explore symptoms and their practical impact. The internal workflow also includes QbCheck, a remote computer-based task used to measure aspects of inattention, impulsivity and hyperactivity in a structured way.

QbCheck does not replace clinical judgement, but it adds another source of information to the wider assessment picture.

## 3. Informant interview

A separate informant interview is also part of the pathway.

Nova Clinics invites someone who knows you well to attend an appointment and share information about your neurodevelopmental history and day-to-day presentation. You are welcome to be present for that discussion as well.

The clinic workflow states that a DIVA/ACE questionnaire is also used in this stage to gather further information about ADHD traits.

## 4. Multidisciplinary team review

Once the assessment information has been collected, the clinicians review it together in a multidisciplinary team meeting.

They consider your questionnaires, interview, informant input, QbCheck findings and overall clinical picture. The team then applies DSM-5 criteria for ADHD to decide whether there is sufficient evidence for a diagnostic outcome.

If the evidence is incomplete or unclear, they may request additional information before confirming the result.

## 5. Report and feedback session

After the team reaches a conclusion, Nova Clinics arranges an online feedback appointment with you.

According to the clinic workflow, this meeting is usually scheduled around two weeks after the multidisciplinary decision, with the written report generally sent one week before the feedback session. That gives you time to read it properly and think through any questions.

## 6. Post-assessment follow-up

Nova Clinics also includes a post-assessment follow-up session, which can be booked within four weeks of the feedback appointment.

This gives you space to discuss the outcome, think about support and practical adjustments, and work through what the assessment means in real life.

That can still be useful even if ADHD is not diagnosed, because the assessment may still clarify where difficulties are showing up and what kinds of support might help.

## How to prepare for an adult ADHD assessment

- choose an informant who knows your everyday functioning well
- note examples of difficulties with focus, organisation or impulsivity
- think about how long those patterns have been present
- gather any relevant school, work or life-history details
- write down questions before the feedback meeting

## Final thought

An adult ADHD assessment is about understanding patterns across time and daily life, not making a snap judgement from one appointment. When you know what each step is for, the process often feels much more manageable.
