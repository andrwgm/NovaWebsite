# Adult combined autism and ADHD assessment: what to expect at each stage

For some adults, the question is not simply whether they may be autistic or may have ADHD. It is whether both sets of traits could be relevant at the same time. That can make the assessment process feel more complicated, especially if you have spent years masking, compensating or being given partial explanations that never fully fit.

At Nova Clinics, the adult combined pathway is designed to assess for autism and ADHD together, using questionnaires, clinical interview, observational work, informant input, multidisciplinary review and follow-up support.

## Why a combined assessment can make sense

Autism and ADHD can overlap in everyday life. Difficulties with focus, energy regulation, social communication, routine, sensory experience and executive functioning may interact in ways that are hard to separate without a structured assessment.

A combined pathway helps clinicians look at the whole pattern rather than approaching each question in isolation.

## 1. Online questionnaires

The first stage is a questionnaire pack completed by you and your informant.

Nova Clinics asks for background information from both of you so the clinicians can begin reviewing your history, current presentation, strengths and difficulties before the live appointments begin.

In the adult workflow, the extra social context questionnaire section is removed, which keeps the focus on your own information and your informant’s perspective.

## 2. Personal interview, observational work and QbCheck

The main clinical stage brings together both autism-focused and ADHD-focused assessment work.

For autism, Nova Clinics uses an ADI-R-informed interview structure based on DSM-5 criteria for autism. The clinic workflow also includes an ADOS-2 observational assessment to evaluate areas such as communication style, social interaction and imaginative thinking.

For ADHD, the pathway includes QbCheck, a remote structured task used to measure aspects of inattention, impulsivity and hyperactivity, alongside a DIVA/ACE-informed interview about ADHD traits and their impact on daily life.

Looking at both profiles together can be especially useful when one set of traits may mask, mimic or complicate the interpretation of the other.

## 3. Informant interview

An informant interview is also part of the combined pathway.

Nova Clinics invites someone who knows you well to share information about your neurodevelopmental history and how you present day to day. The clinic workflow states that this stage uses an autism-focused interview based on DSM-5 criteria and also gathers information about ADHD traits using DIVA/ACE.

You are welcome to attend this appointment too.

## 4. Multidisciplinary team review

After the assessment work is complete, the clinicians review the evidence together in a multidisciplinary team meeting.

They consider your questionnaires, interview, observational work, QbCheck findings and informant information. The team then applies DSM-5 criteria for autism and ADHD to decide whether there is evidence for autism, ADHD, both, or neither.

If more evidence is needed, they may ask for additional information before reaching a final conclusion.

## 5. Report and feedback session

Once the team has reached a conclusion, Nova Clinics arranges an online feedback appointment with you.

The clinic workflow indicates that this is usually held around two weeks after the multidisciplinary decision, with the report normally shared one week beforehand. That timing gives you space to read the report carefully and prepare questions.

For combined assessments, the feedback stage is often where the overall picture becomes clearest, because it explains not just whether criteria are met, but how the different traits may interact.

## 6. Post-assessment follow-up

Nova Clinics includes a post-assessment follow-up session within four weeks of the feedback appointment.

This gives you room to discuss the practical meaning of the outcome, think about support needs, and explore what changes or next steps may be helpful in everyday life.

That conversation can be useful whether the outcome confirms one diagnosis, both, or neither.

## How to prepare for a combined adult assessment

- choose an informant who knows your longer-term patterns well
- note examples linked to both autism and ADHD traits
- think about masking, burnout and executive functioning
- write down questions before the feedback session
- remember that mixed or complex presentations are common

## Final thought

A combined adult assessment is designed to make sense of a more complex profile without reducing it to one label too early. Understanding the stages can make the process feel clearer, steadier and easier to navigate.
