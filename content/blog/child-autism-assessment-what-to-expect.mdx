# Child autism assessment: what to expect at each stage

If you are thinking about a private autism assessment for your child, one of the first questions is usually the simplest one: what actually happens during the process?

At Nova Clinics, the child autism pathway is designed to build a rounded picture of how your child communicates, interacts, develops and manages day-to-day life. Rather than relying on one appointment alone, the assessment brings together questionnaires, parent or carer input, direct observation, multidisciplinary review and follow-up support.

## Why the pathway has several stages

Autism assessments work best when clinicians can look at patterns across more than one setting and from more than one point of view. That is why the process includes both structured forms and live clinical work, rather than a single conversation.

The aim is not to catch a child out or make them perform. It is to understand how they experience the world, what their strengths are, where they may be struggling, and whether the overall evidence meets diagnostic criteria.

## 1. Online questionnaires

The first step is a set of secure online questionnaires about your child’s history, strengths and current challenges.

Nova Clinics also asks for information from your child’s educational setting, such as a class teacher, SENCo or teaching assistant. This helps the clinical team understand how your child presents outside the home as well as within family life.

If your child is not attending school, that does not stop the pathway. Another adult who has known your child for at least six months in a social context may be able to contribute instead, provided consent is in place.

### Why this step matters

These forms give clinicians important background before the live appointments begin. They can highlight recurring themes, identify areas to explore in more depth and help the team prepare a more focused assessment.

## 2. Parent or carer interview

Parents and carers usually hold the clearest picture of a child’s developmental history, so this stage is central to the pathway.

Nova Clinics uses an ADI-R-informed developmental interview. In practical terms, this means the clinician explores your child’s early development, communication, relationships, play, behaviour and current presentation in a structured way.

This is often the stage where parents are asked detailed questions about early milestones, patterns that have been present over time and the situations in which difficulties tend to show up most clearly.

## 3. Child or young person observation

The next stage involves direct observation of your child.

Nova Clinics uses an ADOS-2-informed approach. This is a structured observational format that helps a trained clinician understand communication style, social interaction and, where relevant, play or imaginative use of materials.

The session is not an exam. It is a way of seeing how your child engages, responds and communicates in real time, using activities suited to their developmental stage.

## 4. Multidisciplinary team review

Once the information has been gathered, the clinicians involved in the assessment review it together in a multidisciplinary team meeting.

At this stage, the team considers the questionnaires, parent or carer interview, direct observation and any supporting contextual information. They then use DSM-5 criteria for autism spectrum disorder to decide whether there is sufficient evidence for a diagnostic outcome.

Sometimes the team may ask for further information before reaching a final conclusion. That does not necessarily mean something is wrong; it usually means they want the evidence to be as clear and robust as possible.

## 5. Report and feedback session

After the multidisciplinary review, Nova Clinics arranges an online feedback appointment with the named contact.

According to the clinic workflow, the feedback session takes place around two weeks after the team reaches a conclusion, and the written report is usually shared one week before that conversation. This gives families time to read through the report, absorb the detail and note down questions.

The report is intended to explain the outcome clearly, along with the evidence considered and the clinical reasoning behind it.

## 6. Post-assessment follow-up

The process does not end when the report is delivered.

Nova Clinics includes a post-assessment follow-up session, which can be arranged within four weeks of the feedback appointment. This gives parents and carers space to talk through the outcome, understand what it means in practice and think about next steps.

That follow-up can still be valuable even if the outcome is not an autism diagnosis. Families often still need help making sense of the profile of needs, strengths and support priorities identified during the assessment.

## How to prepare as a parent or carer

- complete forms as fully and honestly as you can
- gather any useful school information in advance
- note examples of day-to-day strengths and difficulties
- write down questions before the feedback appointment
- remember that uncertainty at the start is completely normal

## Final thought

A child autism assessment is not just a single appointment. It is a staged process designed to build a careful, evidence-based understanding of your child. Knowing what each step is for can make the pathway feel much less opaque and help you prepare with more confidence.
