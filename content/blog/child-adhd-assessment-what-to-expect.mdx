# Child ADHD assessment: what to expect at each stage

Families often come to an ADHD assessment with a long list of questions: what happens first, will school be involved, and what does the clinician need from us?

At Nova Clinics, the child ADHD pathway is designed to gather evidence from more than one source and in more than one format. That matters because ADHD is not identified through a single checklist alone. Clinicians need to understand patterns of attention, impulsivity, activity level, development and day-to-day impact over time.

## Why the process includes more than one appointment

A thorough ADHD assessment looks beyond whether a child is busy or struggles to concentrate on some tasks. It asks how consistent the pattern is, where it shows up, how long it has been present and what effect it is having at home, in education and in everyday functioning.

That is why the pathway combines questionnaires, developmental history, direct clinical contact, structured ADHD tools and multidisciplinary review.

## 1. Online questionnaires

The first stage is a set of secure online questionnaires covering your child’s history, strengths and current challenges.

Nova Clinics also gathers information from your child’s educational setting, usually through a teacher, SENCo or teaching assistant. This helps the team compare what is seen at home with what is happening in school or another learning environment.

If your child is not in school, the clinic can use information from another adult who has known them for at least six months in a social context, with consent.

### Why this matters

ADHD traits can look different depending on the setting. School or educational feedback can help clinicians understand whether difficulties with attention, impulsivity or hyperactivity are present across contexts rather than in only one environment.

## 2. Parent or carer developmental interview

Parents and carers play a key role in the assessment because they usually provide the clearest picture of how concerns have developed over time.

Nova Clinics uses a DIVA/ACE Plus-informed approach alongside a developmental history interview. This helps clinicians explore early presentation, ongoing patterns and the practical impact of symptoms on everyday life.

The goal here is not simply to identify classic ADHD behaviours, but to understand how your child functions, what they find hard, where they cope well and what support they may need.

## 3. Child observation and QbCheck

The clinical pathway also includes direct contact with your child or young person.

A key part of the ADHD process is the QbCheck, a computer-based task used remotely through a web browser. According to the clinic pathway, it usually takes around 20 minutes to complete and is used to assess aspects of inattention, impulsivity and hyperactivity in a structured way.

Alongside QbCheck, the clinical team meets with your child to understand their strengths, needs and day-to-day experience. A DIVA/ACE-informed discussion may also be used to gather more information about ADHD traits and how they affect daily life.

## 4. Multidisciplinary team review

Once the assessment information has been collected, the clinicians review it together in a multidisciplinary team meeting.

They consider the questionnaires, developmental history, school or contextual information, QbCheck findings and clinical observations. The team then uses DSM-5 criteria for ADHD to decide whether the available evidence supports a diagnosis.

If more information is needed, the team may ask for it before confirming an outcome.

## 5. Report and feedback session

After the multidisciplinary review, Nova Clinics arranges a feedback session with the named contact.

The clinic workflow states that this meeting usually takes place around two weeks after the team reaches a conclusion, with the written report normally sent one week beforehand. That gives families time to read the report and prepare questions.

This stage is important because it turns the assessment from a set of appointments into a clear explanation of the outcome and what it means.

## 6. Post-assessment follow-up

Nova Clinics also includes a post-assessment follow-up session, available within four weeks of the feedback appointment.

This gives families a chance to talk through the outcome, understand their child’s profile in more practical terms and think about support, advocacy and next steps. That can be useful whether or not the final outcome is ADHD.

## How to prepare for the pathway

- complete questionnaires with concrete examples
- gather relevant school reports or notes
- think about when concerns first appeared
- note where difficulties are most noticeable
- write down questions for the feedback appointment

## Final thought

A child ADHD assessment is not about one score or one conversation. It is a structured pathway designed to understand your child’s functioning across settings and over time. Knowing how the process works can make the whole experience feel clearer and less overwhelming.
