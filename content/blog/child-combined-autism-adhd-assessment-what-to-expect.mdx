# Child combined autism and ADHD assessment: what to expect at each stage

Sometimes families are not just wondering about autism or ADHD separately. They are trying to understand a child whose profile seems to include features of both. In those cases, a combined assessment pathway can be more practical and more coherent than exploring each condition in isolation.

At Nova Clinics, the combined child pathway is designed to assess for autism and ADHD together, using a staged process that brings together questionnaires, developmental history, direct observation, ADHD-specific testing, multidisciplinary review and follow-up support.

## Why a combined pathway can be useful

Autism and ADHD can overlap in ways that make the picture harder to untangle. A child may have social communication differences, sensory sensitivities or preference for routine alongside difficulties with attention, impulsivity or activity level.

A combined pathway helps clinicians look at the whole profile rather than forcing families into an either-or framework too early.

## 1. Online questionnaires

The process starts with secure online questionnaires about your child’s history, strengths and current challenges.

Nova Clinics also seeks information from your child’s educational setting, usually through a teacher, SENCo or teaching assistant. If your child is not in school, another adult who has known them for at least six months in a social setting may be able to provide supporting information instead, with consent.

This stage gives the clinical team an early view of how your child presents across settings and where the main questions lie.

## 2. Parent or carer interview

The parent or carer interview is one of the most important parts of the pathway.

For the autism side of the assessment, Nova Clinics uses an ADI-R-informed developmental interview to explore early development, communication, social relationships and broader developmental history.

For the ADHD side, the clinic also uses a DIVA/ACE Plus-informed interview to explore attention, impulsivity, hyperactivity and their effect on day-to-day life.

Taken together, these conversations help the team build a fuller developmental picture without artificially separating concerns that may coexist.

## 3. Child observation and ADHD testing

Direct clinical work with your child forms the next stage.

For autism, Nova Clinics uses an ADOS-2-informed observational format to understand communication style, social interaction and, where appropriate, play or imaginative use of materials.

For ADHD, the clinic includes QbCheck, a computer-based remote assessment that measures aspects of inattention, impulsivity and hyperactivity. The clinical team also meets with your child to understand their strengths, needs and everyday presentation more broadly.

This combined stage is particularly helpful when behaviours that look similar on the surface may have different underlying explanations.

## 4. Multidisciplinary team review

After the information has been gathered, the clinicians review the full evidence together in a multidisciplinary team meeting.

The team considers all assessment components and then applies DSM-5 criteria for autism spectrum disorder and ADHD. The aim is to determine whether there is evidence for autism, ADHD, both, or neither.

If the picture remains unclear, the team may request further information before making a final decision.

## 5. Report and feedback session

Once the team has reached a conclusion, Nova Clinics arranges an online feedback appointment with the named contact.

Based on the clinic workflow, the feedback meeting usually happens around two weeks after the multidisciplinary decision, and the report is generally sent one week before that appointment. This gives families time to process the written information and prepare questions.

For combined assessments, this stage can be especially valuable because families often need help understanding how the two profiles interact rather than seeing them as separate boxes.

## 6. Post-assessment follow-up

The pathway also includes a post-assessment follow-up session, which can be booked within four weeks of the feedback appointment.

This creates space to talk through the outcome, support needs and next steps in more practical terms. Even where a diagnosis is not confirmed, the follow-up can help families better understand the child’s profile and think about support and advocacy.

## How families can prepare

- note examples that suggest autism-related differences
- note examples that suggest attention or activity-related difficulties
- gather school feedback in advance
- be ready to describe developmental history over time
- keep a list of questions for the report and feedback stage

## Final thought

A combined assessment can feel like a big step, but it can also be the clearest route when a child’s presentation does not fit neatly into one category. A good pathway helps clinicians understand the whole child, not just isolated traits.
