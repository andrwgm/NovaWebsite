# Adult autism assessment: what to expect at each stage

Many adults start exploring autism after years of feeling different, misunderstood or exhausted by trying to cope without a clear explanation. Once you decide to seek an assessment, the next question is usually practical: what does the pathway actually involve?

At Nova Clinics, the adult autism pathway is designed to build an evidence-based picture of your developmental history and current presentation through questionnaires, clinical interview, informant input, multidisciplinary review and follow-up support.

## Why adult autism assessments use more than one source of information

Autism is a developmental condition, which means clinicians usually need to understand both current traits and the longer-term pattern behind them. That can be harder in adulthood, especially if you have developed coping strategies or have spent years masking.

For that reason, the pathway does not rely only on self-report. It also draws on informant information from someone who knows you well.

## 1. Online questionnaires

The pathway begins with questionnaires completed by you and your informant.

Nova Clinics asks you to provide background information and asks someone who knows you well to complete forms as well. In the clinic workflow, this informant is a key source of contextual information about your day-to-day presentation and developmental history.

The adult version of the internal workflow specifically removes the broader social context questionnaire section, so the focus is on you and your chosen informant rather than an additional outside contributor.

## 2. Personal interview and observational work

The next stage is your main clinical interview.

Nova Clinics uses an ADI-R-informed interview structure alongside DSM-5 criteria for autism to explore your current presentation and relevant developmental patterns. In practice, that means the clinician asks detailed questions about communication, social experience, routines, sensory differences and how traits affect daily life.

The clinic workflow also states that this stage includes an ADOS-2 observational assessment. This is a structured clinical tool used to evaluate areas such as communication style, social interaction and imaginative thinking or use of materials.

## 3. Informant interview

An informant interview is a separate part of the pathway.

Nova Clinics invites someone who knows you well to attend an appointment and share their perspective on your neurodevelopmental history and day-to-day presentation. You are welcome to attend this conversation too.

This stage is often useful because adults may not remember early developmental details clearly, or may view long-standing patterns as simply normal for me. Informant input can help fill in those gaps.

## 4. Multidisciplinary team review

After the appointments are complete, the clinicians involved review the evidence together in a multidisciplinary team meeting.

They consider the questionnaires, your interview, the observational assessment and the informant interview. The team then applies DSM-5 criteria for autism spectrum disorder to decide whether the available evidence supports a diagnosis.

If further information is needed, the team may request it before finalising the outcome.

## 5. Report and feedback session

Once the team has reached a conclusion, Nova Clinics arranges an online feedback appointment with you.

Based on the clinic workflow, this usually happens around two weeks after the multidisciplinary decision, and the written report is normally sent one week beforehand. That gives you time to read the report, process the information and prepare any questions.

For many adults, this stage can be emotionally significant. Even when the outcome is not what they expected, having a structured explanation of the evidence can still be helpful.

## 6. Post-assessment follow-up

Nova Clinics also includes a post-assessment follow-up session, which can be arranged within four weeks of the feedback appointment.

This creates space to discuss what the outcome means in everyday terms, what support or adjustments may be helpful and what next steps you may want to consider.

That follow-up can still be useful even if autism is not diagnosed, because the assessment may still clarify important aspects of your needs and functioning.

## How to prepare for an adult autism assessment

- choose an informant who knows you well
- make notes about patterns from childhood to adulthood
- think about masking, burnout and sensory experience
- note examples of social, communication or routine-related difficulties
- write down questions for the feedback session

## Final thought

An adult autism assessment is a structured process, not a single opinion formed in one conversation. Understanding each stage can make the pathway feel less uncertain and help you take part in it with more confidence.
