## Introduction

Adult ADHD is often described as a “focus problem,” but many people experience it as a **systems problem**: starting tasks, switching tasks, estimating time, recovering after interruptions, and keeping emotional momentum steady when demands pile up.

The strategies below are not about becoming a different person. They are about building environments that make your strengths easier to access.

## Start with energy, not guilt

If your week feels like a series of rescue missions, the first step is not a new app—it is **protecting baseline needs**: sleep, food, movement, medication consistency (if prescribed), and predictable recovery time.

## Make the first step embarrassingly small

“Write the report” is not a first step. A first step might be:

- Open the document
- Write a deliberately bad title
- Paste bullet notes from your phone

Momentum is easier to steer than it is to summon from zero.

## Boundaries are accessibility

Many adults with ADHD are skilled at urgent work—and then pay for it later. Boundaries are not selfish; they are **cognitive accessibility**: fewer interruptions, clearer priorities, and fewer context switches.

## Closing thoughts

If you have wondered whether a formal assessment could clarify your profile, you are not starting late. Understanding can arrive at any age—and it often helps more than “trying harder.”
